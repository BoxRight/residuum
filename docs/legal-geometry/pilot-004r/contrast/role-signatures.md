**Reading.**
- h1 and h2 differ in exactly two positions: h2 swaps `subject` and
  `beneficiary`.
- LegalGeneral fixes the operational role of both arguments in `right` and in
  `obligation`. `subject` is the party whose `Act` (or `~Act`) counts for
  compliance, enforceability or violation, and `beneficiary` is the
  beneficiary of that act.
- LegalGeneral does not say who **holds** the `right`. No rule reads a holder.
  A description such as "creditor rights" for h1 is therefore consistent with
  the theory if the holder is read as the `beneficiary`. It is inconsistent
  only if the holder is read as the `subject`. The theory does not settle
  this; it is a convention to declare.
- In operational terms, h1 keeps the performer: the `right` binds the same
  party the obligation binds. h2 makes the creditor the performer of the
  conduct owed by the debtor.
